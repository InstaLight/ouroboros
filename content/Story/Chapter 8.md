Willow wisps danced among the starlight. Fine grains of sand spread across the vast abyss. Fireflies illuminating a dark theater. Static from an old TV, its crackling filling the silence on a warm summer night. Birds chirp, yet no one responds. Light moving impossibly fast, only to be brought to a halt a second later. A warm breeze spread throughout the arctic caps. Drowning in an endless ocean, constantly sinking, unable to float.  
The dream began to stabilize. Mark stood once again in front of the orb, closer than ever before, its radiance brighter than it had been. A sense of lucidity washed over him as he examined his surroundings. It was just like last time, the bizarre dreams playing out behind him, with the only clear path being forward. Something was different, however. Mark could move normally, no longer restricted by the thick invisible sludge from last time. He began walking towards the orb, finally able to make progress. The closer he got, the stronger the feeling became. That feeling when you know someone is watching you, even when you're all alone. He could sense someone just out of view, their eyes locked onto him.  
"Don't look," he thought to himself. If he looked, he knew the dream would end and he would be right back to square one.  
Something behind him shifted. The dream began to change yet again, but something was off. The soft glow from beyond the door began to dim, and Mark began to hear the faint sounds of waves crashing. The flames of the orb began to brighten, as though it was fighting back against the waves, warding off the surrounding darkness. Mark's pace began to quicken, and before he knew it he was in a full on sprint. The hinges of the door began to squeak, the water piling up, slamming repeatedly into it. The eyes continued to follow, the pit in Mark's stomach growing with each stride.  
"Only 400 meters away, one last sprint and I'm there."  
The door broke open, the waves crashing through as though they were trying to race Mark. He could feel a cool breeze coming off them as they quickly caught up, the droplets grabbing for Mark's back.

Mark jumped, his left hand reaching out to grab hold of the orb. The waves grazed his foot, their strength pulling Mark back. He outstretched out arm as far as it would go, his finger tip just barely making contact with the orb. It burned, the same burn that he felt when he would ignite his blood. The silence came rushing back. The waves ceased to exist. The dreams resumed. Mark got up and placed both hands on the orb, ready to accept whatever pain would follow. It felt as though he held the sun in the palm of his hands. He could only hold on for a split second. A soft, warm voice filled his mind. A woman's voice, motherly in nature, spoke to him.  
"Hello Mark, my name is-"  
He shot back, unable to bear the flames any further. He looked up. Those eyes. Those deep, indigo-  
The roar of an old desktops fans. The cricket that sits by your window. The radio playing the same tune. The brother that isn't real. The girl that you never met, and never will meet. Not in this life, at least.\

---

Mark sat by his front door, petting Nara's head as he waited for Vincent to come and pick him up. His mom looked more tired than usual, and she downed her second cup of the coffee. "Must be something in the air," Mark thought to himself, as he too felt a peculiar sense of drowsiness.  
"How are you feeling mom?" he said, his mom jumping, appearing caught off guard by the question.  
"Me? I'm just fine, thank you for asking, love." she walked over and kissed him on the forehead, a strange sadness lingering in her eyes.  
"Are you sure? You don't seem like you're doing too hot."  
She gave a weak smile, "It's nothing," she glanced over at the picture of his father, her husband, hanging on the wall. "I'm just missing him a little extra today."  
Mark stood up and gave his mom a hug, the two lingering in each other's arms. Three quick, obnoxious honks came from outside, notifying Mark that his ride had arrived.  
"See you after school, okay?" His mom said, pulling him back for one last hug.

---

The three boys sat at their lunch table as usual, a sense of dread hanging over Mark.  
"Why do you look like you just failed all your classes?" Tim asked, a concerned look on his face.  
"Maybe our boy is just upset about his lady problems," Vincent snarked, flicking a piece of paper at Mark's forehead, a piece that he promptly chucked back.  
"I don't have 'lady problems'," he replied, "it's all this extra shit Coach is assigning me, it's really bumming me out. He gave me a damn diet for crying out loud. What's the point of all this extra bullshit if I'm already the best on the team." He looked off to the side, spotting Molly sitting with her usual crew. She caught his gaze, giving him a quick smile and wave. Mark looked back at his table, face beet-red as usual.  
Vincent looked over his shoulder, and upon seeing the beaming Molly, he turned back and began chucking. "Yeah man, you're clearly thinking about your new diet."  
"Maybe he just wants to make sure it wasn't a one time fluke!" Tim chimed in, before quickly following-up, "Not saying that it was! Just that he might think that."  
A small crowd began to form at a table nearby, the volume in that direction increasing.  
"Ok, ok, enough about sports and running and girls," Vincent said, changing the subject, "What did you guys think of that new Spider-man trailer? An animated movie about Miles Morales is going to be awesome!"  
"Holy shit!" a boy behind them shouted, the crowd growing rapidly. The commotion was getting too crazy to ignore, and the trio walked over to investigate. Mark pushed through the hoard of classmates to see an iPad sitting at the center of a table, the news playing with its volume showed up. Mark's heart skipped a beat. Though he couldn't see him, he could tell Vincent had tensed up as well. The scene on the news played out like something from an early morning cartoon. A distressed reporter. Helicopter footage. A crime occurring downtown. What should've been a normal report on a bank robbery turned into something much greater. Phones began to blow up. Teachers came over, staying nothing as they watched the scene unfold. The camera zoomed in on a single man, standing in front of a line of officers, all with their guns drawn. Behind the man stood someone else, who was assumed to be his accomplice. The man's body appeared as though it was shimmering, a liquid secreting off of him. The accomplice ran back inside, and the police began to fire. The bullets filled the man's body, his accomplice making it inside, avoiding any damage from the onslaught. Students gasped. Mark could hear his own breathing. No one was prepared for what happened next. When the officers stopped firing, the man remained still, somehow standing after all of the bullets. Slowly, the man lifted his arm, and performed a simple flick with two of his fingers. A shock wave burst forward, flinging the officers back, their cars going flying. Windows shattered, and even the helicopter began to shake as it felt the outburst. People began to scream as the feed went back to the reporter. Others began saying how cool it was. Mark looked back at Vincent, and a sense of understanding was felt between them. This is what the young boy was talking about. The show had begun.  
Twelve murders. Two counts of grand theft auto. Four banks were robbed. One kidnapping. Twenty counts of arson. Hundreds of sightings of "gifted" individuals. Mark sat through class, constantly refreshing his Twitter feed. Shortly into his first class after lunch, the principal made an announcement, telling everyone that school would be closed for the remainder of the week as this "situation" unfolded. People began to rush to their cars, trying to leave as soon as possible. Mark said his goodbyes to Tim before braving the terror that was the high school hallway. He worked through the crowd of people, eventually spotted Vincent who was holding up his car keys, motioning Mark to follow. Mark felt someone grab his wrist, pulling him out of the crowd and into an empty classroom. Molly stood in front of him, tears in her eyes. Before he could say anything, she rushed forward and hugged him.  
"Do you have a ride home?" he asked, unsure of how to handle this.  
"Yeah, I'm just so scared, what does all of this even mean?"  
He looked away. He felt bad lying, but he couldn't tell her anything. "I don't know, but it's all going to be okay I promise." He grabbed her face, forcing her to lock eyes with him. "I'm sorry but I really have to go, if you need me I'm always right down the street. Please get home safe." He rushed out the door before she could respond. She stood in the empty classroom, her cheeks slightly red.

---

"You are one bad luck charm!" Vincent shouted, going 90mph on the highway. "I mean all I got was some poor people forced to attack me, but somehow you getting your powers causes some sick chain reaction. Do we know if this is worldwide?"  
"The news is everywhere at this point," Mark responded, eyes glued to his phone, "but I think the individuals are mostly contained to our area. The farthest I've seen someone is Allentown, which is only about an hour away."  
"Ok then, that basically confirms my suspicion, they're probably connected to us, and that asshole in the suit somehow." A grin began to spread on Vincent's face, as he turned up the volume of his music. Mark went pale.  
"I don't like that face, I know it means something and I don't like it."  
Vincent continued to rock out, occasionally glancing at Mark with a sly look.  
"You know what this means, right?" Vincent finally said. Mark said nothing, as he continued to stare at Vincent. "Well I guess I'll be the one to say it. We have to become super-heroes."  
"Nope, fuck off, not doing it."  
Vincent swerved between traffic, causing some angry drivers to blast their horns. He was unfazed. "Listen to me Mark, if we wanna get this guy and end all this nonsense, we gotta take down his minions. Defeat the mobs to get to the boss, come on this is basic stuff."  
"This is actually your dumbest idea yet, do you even have a brain in there? We are teenagers with powers that are beyond our understanding that honestly, do more harm than good, and you want to go around playing super-hero?"  
Vincent just smiled in response.  
Mark put his head against the headrest and closed his eyes. He ran his fingers over the crystal through his shirt, feeling its warmth.  
"I know you wanna be a super-hero too. I'm sureeeee Molly would be impressed too."  
Mark side-eyed Vincent, who was still grinning from cheek to cheek. Mark yelled into his hands, before dropping them and sighing.\
"We need super hero identities. And no lame costumes. I'll kill you if you make me wear a lame costume."
