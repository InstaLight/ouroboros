Mark collapsed onto his bed, the exhaustion of the past couple of days catching up to him. Ever since his horrendous performance at the invitational, Coach had been working him like a dog. When Mark crossed that finish line, Coach completely collapsed, Tim frantically looking for any kind of medic to assist. Mark was almost back to being a nobody at school, his slight fame already faded. Coach, miraculously, hadn't given up on Mark yet, and was in a manic state writing new training plans for him. By the time Mark gets home at night, his mom is already passed out, with leftovers and a nice note on the table waiting for him. His mom seemed to be getting less and less sleep every night, to the point where Mark had to wake her up before he left for school the other day. He tried to not let his mind go to the worst possibility, but it was impossible to know for sure if she would turn into an oddity like him. Nara on the other hand was always wide awake when Mark would arrive home, practically tackling him as he got through the door. On top of the intensive cross country training, Mark and Vincent were now averaging around 2.5 takedowns a day, which was becoming increasingly troublesome for Mark's work-life balance. Vincent had suggested Mark just quit cross country, at least until this whole thing ended, but he refused. On top of just loving the sport, he knew that Tim would be crushed if Mark left him alone on the team. So in the end, Mark had no one to blame for his lack of free time but himself. In the fleeting moments alone that Mark did have, he began to develop an app for him and Vincent to keep track of everything. The app worked like a ticketing system, taking in publicly available police calls in a designated area, turning each call into a ticket, and then organizing each ticket by severity. The system would allow the two to not only get instant alerts to their phones instead of having to stalk social media, but also allowed them to log each oddity they went up against, growing a kind of Pokédex of people. Today, however, was a Friday, which meant one thing: Mark was not doing shit for the next hour.  
With Nara licking his leg, Mark pulled out his laptop from under his bed and went to Twitter. While he never really got thanks for his heroics in person, mostly due to each takedown ending with police trying to shoot him and his partner in crime, social media would often have the praise that he was looking for. He would always see one or two stories from someone, and how they spotted the pair in action. The general public still couldn't decide on a good name for the two, although Mark and Vincent had finally decided on a name themselves earlier that week. On top of just general praise, there was also some valid criticism in there that Mark would log down in his notes app. Comments calling him "reckless" or "scary" were pretty common, but that was something he would have to work on overtime. His favorite posts, however, were fanart. The hundreds of drawings of him in different art styles made him feel a special way (except that one of him and Vincent he saw on tumblr), and he had a special collection of his favorite ones printed out and hung on his wall. His favorite was one clearly drawn by a kid, done entirely in red crayon. The figure on the paper looked nothing like him, instead looking like something out of Minecraft, but the intention was there.  
A text appeared on the top right of Mark's laptop, the loud ding grabbing his attention. It was Vincent, asking Mark if he wanted to run over and spend the night playing Halo. As enticing as that sounded, Mark moved his cursor and dismissed the text, telling himself he would respond later. I just need one hour, he thought. One hour and I'll be good to go. His eye lids felt like they were being pulled down by 100lb weights, not even allowing him to keep them open. Before he realized it, his consciousness faded, and his sleeping mind took over.  
Mark's body began to sink, sinking deep into an abyss of nothing. Above him, the sun raged, its heat penetrating even the darkest depths of the ocean. The cold darkness caressed his back, trying to wrap its slimy arms around him. The two powers fought over his body, as Mark laid there, unresponsive, letting the best one win. The darkness began to drag him down deeper, his brain growing even foggier, almost like he was about to fall asleep within his dream. The darkness began to coil around his body, shielding him from the blazing sun. Though it was cold, it brought comfort, comfort that the sun was never really able to provide. The sun would heal him and give him strength, sure, but it always came at a cost. The darkness, on the other hand, asked for nothing. The darkness, outstretching its hand, reached for the crystal. Extending a single finger, the darkness went to give the object a light tap, just to see-  
The water around Mark pushed backwards, as though someone placed a large cylinder around him and removed all the water inside. The darkness was pushed away, the full rage of the sun piercing the concept. Mark felt his body rising upwards, getting closer and closer to the blinding light. The familiar burning returned, all sense of comfort evaporating. His body began to press against the sun, his flesh burning before contact was even made. He tried to scream, but there was no air in his lungs to expend. His mind now clear, he knew for a fact he was in a dream. Despite this, the pain itself was very real, at least in this moment, and he wished for nothing more than to wake up. He sunk deeper into the sun, his body engulfed in flames. How could one bear this pain for this long and not wake up? He tried to cry, until he realized he no longer had eyes. He no longer had skin. He no longer had touch. He couldn't even hear the sun's roar, or smell his flesh burning. He began to feel nothing.  
Suddenly, he was there. Everything had returned to him, and he found himself standing back in that room. Behind him, the door from the previous dream was still busted open, though water no longer flowed through the room. Directly in front of him was the orb, the orb that he had barely been able to hold onto for more than half a second previously. Most importantly of all, that feeling had returned. He was being watched again.  
With no other options, Mark reached out with both hands and grabbed the orb. The burning had once again returned, though not as strong. The pain was still immense, but either through its mercy or his ever increasing strength, he would be able to hold on for slightly longer.  
"You tried to speak to me last time," Mark's voice quivered as he fought back from screaming. "Please, tell me your name."  
The orb began to glow, and though it had no mouth to speak with, its words came out clear as day. "My name is Solaris, and it's nice to finally get a chance to speak to you. For some reason, my words can only reach you when your mind is in this stasis."  
Before Mark could respond, the orb began to resonate again. "Before you ask any further questions, I'm afraid I have unfortunate news for you: I do not remember anything beyond my name. The rest of my memories have seemingly been shattered."  
Mark's hands began to bubble, his time was running out. "I still have to ask," he started, his arms beginning to shake. "does the name Nightmare ring any bells whatsoever?"  
Though he could not see them, Mark felt the watching eyes widen.  
"I am unfamiliar with the name," the orb spoke, "however, I can tell you this. Something is trying to get to you, not just externally, but internally as well. I have been able to hold it back for now, but I fear what may happen if it claims you." Flames began to work their way up Mark's arms, using his body as fuel.  
Mark didn't care about trying to look tough anymore. He began to scream, falling to his knees as he fought to ask his final question. "Whose damn eyes are watching us?!" He had to let go any second now.  
"He is hope." Solaris said, a sense of admiration in its voice.  
Mark wanted desperately to keep talking, to keep asking the mysterious life form questions, but he could hold out for no longer. He released the orb from his grasp, gasping as he fully collapsed. The orb faded away, a single ember falling as it disappeared. The eyes shut, the overwhelming presence suddenly vanishing. Mark was alone in his mind, with only the sounds of his own heavy breath to keep him company. He began to sink into the ground, allowing his mind to go dark as true dreams took hold.

Andrew sobbed, the pain being too much for him. "Mommy!" he cried out, unable to see.  
"The human body is quite remarkable," the man said, running his fingers across the jet-black crystal embedded in Andrew's wrist. "You're all filled with so much potential, you just need some help unlocking it." He began to press down on the crystal, Andrews screams amplifying. "We're going to see how hard it is to break some limits, okay?"  
"I just want to go home, please I just want to see my mom again." Andrew pleaded, snot dripping. "Please, Nightmare."  
Nightmare tsked, mocking the boy. "Oh but you were so excited when we first started. Don't you still want payback on Mark? He ruined everything didn't he?"  
Andrew began to shake violently, pulling to break free from his restraints.  
Nightmares' ever-persistent smile grew. "That's what I thought. It's almost time for the party!"

Vincent checked his phone for the twelfth time that hour, once again seeing the no new notifications status. "That asshole." he said aloud to himself. "I guess I can't be too much of a hater, he had a rough week." Vincent popped an orange slice into his mouth, pacing around his room before eventually deciding to sit on the floor. "Halo, or self improvement? Halo, or self improvement?" he motioned his hands up and down, as if they were two sides of a scale. "Gah, Halos no fun solo." he sighed, getting up and walking over to his CD collection. He scanned the rack, looking for the perfect album for a night alone. After a moment of deliberation, he popped one out and placed it into his player. Sitting back on the floor, he began to ground himself. He placed his palms on the ground, allowing them to feel all of the movement in the house. He felt his two sisters playing in their room together, his dad passed out on the couch after a 12 hour shift. He extended his reach to beyond the house, feeling the earth in the surrounding area. He removed all thoughts from his mind, letting the music guide him. He could feel into other people's houses, he could feel the insides of trees and the squirrels that slept within, and he could feel the flowers buried in gardens. Vincent's favorite song on the album came on, bringing him back to his room. He grinned, raised his hands, and slammed them together, the two halves of the crystal completing each other. "Okay Terra, let's talk."
