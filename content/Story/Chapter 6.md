He wasn't threatening. He wasn't saying anything, making any motions. He was just standing there, a smile on his face. This stirred something deep inside of Mark, his crystal's light penetrating his shirt. The man locked eyes with him, before turning and beginning to walk away. Mark broke out into a sprint, the 2 weeks of cross country practice paying off surprisingly well. Within two seconds, he was an arms length away from the man. Mark outstretched his hand, ready to attack, before the realization hit him like a truck. He couldn't use his powers. He had no way to draw blood. He's running head first at what could potentially be his new arch nemesis and he had no way to fight. The man slowly turned, a toothy smile permanently etched on his face, his presence completely overwhelming Mark. Mark froze, physically unable to move. It felt as though the man was the size of a skyscraper, looming over Mark like he would crush him at any moment. The man outstretched his arm, his hand inches away from Mark's chest, before performing a simple swiping motion. The movement had no flair, it was a simple gesture. Mark flew backwards, his body rocketing across the street. He couldn't feel his legs. Oh my god, his legs. Every part of him below his torso was split, a clean cut separating his body in two. His upper body landed with a thud, all of the wind knocked out of him. He gasped for air, but no matter how much he took in, none of it filled his lungs. For a moment, he lost consciousness.  
Mark jolted up, sweat dripping down his face, eyes moving frantically. He looked down to see... his torso, still perfectly intact. In fact, there wasn't a scratch on him. "Is this due to the crystal?" he wondered, running his hands across his body, looking for any sign of damage. His clothes were still in perfect condition, no blood staining them. "Was it all in my head?" Mark looked around, scanning the area for the man. He was nowhere to be seen. Standing up, Mark jogged down the street to his house, once again surveilling the area before entering. His mom was sitting on the couch, sipping on a glass of wine and watching her show of the week, while Nara laid on her back next to her.  
"Hey sweetie, how was school and practice today? Are you ready for the meet tomorrow?"  
Seeing she was okay, Mark quickly walked past, making his way upstairs. "Yeah mom everything was great, super tired though, gotta get my rest before the race." He shouted, before locking himself in his room. He fished his phone out of his pocket, dialing Vincent's number.  
"Yo," Vincent picked up. His sisters could be heard arguing in the background "what's up I just saw you like ten minutes ago, you can't miss me that much already."  
"Sorry to bother, I just wanted to check in to make sure everything is ok over there."  
"Mark, if something happened I need to know." Vincent's tone grew serious.  
"Sorry, forget I said anything, I'll see you tomorrow," Mark quickly hung up the phone before Vincent could respond.  
Mark collapsed onto his bed, burying his face into his hands. One week. He was able to go one week without bullshit happening.

"Are you nervous for your first race?" Tim asked, eyes wide, not even looking at Mark.  
Mark chuckled, "Clearly not as nervous as you are."  
"Oh please, I ran cross country all throughout middle school, this is nothing." Tim was literally bouncing up and down with nervous energy.  
The pair were standing on the starting line, only minutes away from their first race of the season. People surrounded the starting line, cheers coming from all directions. In the mass of people, Mark spotted Molly and her posse, all wearing blue and gold, their school colors, cheering. Mark refused to make eye contact with her. He looked around, trying to spot Vincent, before getting a nudge on the shoulder from Tim.  
"Lock in," he whispered. "Just stick with me and we'll do great."  
Mark looked ahead, noticing that the starter was holding his gun up in the air. It was at this moment that Mark realized how nervous he was. Despite everything he had been through recently, it felt like this was the most stressful event in his life. So many people were watching, what if he embarrassed himself in front of all of them? The gun fired, and the starter lowered his flag. Mark's body moved on instinct, he didn't even realize he began to run. Tim looked up at him, smiling, before focusing back on the race. Mark's mind was running faster than he was, he was unable to pick out a single concrete thought and focus on it. He thought of the most random things, like what he had for lunch 3 days ago, the smell of Christmas, how he couldn't wait to take a nap after this. Then, finally, it came. The one singular thought that he could focus on. It was a song. One he had found on Spotify a couple of nights ago, and that he'd been playing on loop since he first played it. "Speed Racer" by Hers began to play on repeat in his mind. He completely zoned out, the world around him becoming a blur of colors. The sounds around him became muffled, as if someone was trying to talk to him through a solid wall of concrete. His legs continued to push forward on their own. By the time he had run through the song seven times in his head, he snapped back to reality. The finish line was just up ahead. The roars of the crowd came crashing in. He couldn't even feel his legs, but he kept pushing until he crossed the finish. He collapsed at the end, his coach running up to him to grab him.  
"How bad did I do, Coach?"  
Coach looked like his eyes were about to pop out of their sockets. "Are you fucking kidding me McQuaid? That was the fastest 5k I've ever seen out of this school."  
Mark, confused, looked around. He was the only runner there. He glanced at the timer, almost throwing up at the sight. He finished the 5k in 15 minutes and 12 seconds.  
Mark waited for Tim to cross the finish, shaking his head in disbelief as they hugged each other.  
"Where the hell did that come from?" Tim asked, looking at Mark like he was some kind of super hero.  
"I really could not tell you," Mark responded. For the past 5 minutes, random people who Mark had never even seen before were coming up to him, congratulating him and giving him high fives. Tim leaned in close, his arm clasped onto Mark's shoulder. "Hey man, I think you got a fan club."  
Mark looked over in the direction that Tim was staring, only to lock eyes with Molly, who was smiling and holding a big thumbs up in the air. Mark quickly redirected his gaze towards the ground, his face burning red with embarrassment. Suddenly, a big pair of arms grabbed Mark and Tim by their necks, pulling them into a bear hug.  
"That was some mighty fine running out there gentlemen," Vincent said in one of his terrible fake accents. "I'll need to play some bets at the next derby!"  
Vincent looked at Mark, his tone changing entirely. "My house, two hours, meet me in the backyard." he said in a hushed voice, before turning to Tim. "Say Timmy, I'll pay for any treat you want, as long as it's less than $3."

---

Mark stepped into Vincent's backyard, still riding off his high from earlier. Before leaving the meet, his coach met his mom and began telling her what a special son she has, before handing off a quickly written list containing a carefully considered meal plan to keep him in top shape. His coach then proceeded to tell Mark that he would have to start doing special workouts on the weekend, before he walked off muttering something about "going all the way to the olympics." Mark wasn't too sure he liked being good at running. Vincent sat underneath one of the trees, meditating, calling out to Mark, "You saw him yesterday didn't you."  
Mark froze, unsure of how to respond. "I don't know what-"  
"Stop. You saw him and you didn't tell me, that's a messed up man."  
"I didn't want to worry you, besides he was gone before I even called you."  
"So you thought that asking if I was okay all panicked and then hanging up the phone wouldn't worry me? He was still nearby, he could've come here, to my house, where my family lives."  
Mark winced. He didn't think about it like that.  
"In fact," Vincent said, standing up, "how the hell did he even know where you were? He has never gotten this close to this area, and now all of a sudden when you get your powers he's showing up right down the block?"  
"Hey man, what are you trying to say? Do you think I'm with him or something, because I'll tell you right now that that is bullshit. Besides, you said he would show up at random places you were at, meaning he knows where you are to some extent as well, right?"  
Vincent stopped, taking a deep breath, and running his hand through his hair. "You're right, sorry if I came off as aggressive, I just don't mess around when it comes to my family."  
"It's okay man I understand." The two gave each other a look of understanding, before Vincent jumped back to his usual hyperactive self. "Okay so, last night after I deduced what your call was about I began to brainstorm. Clearly this man in the suit is up to no good, and is somehow connected to these crystals. And, because we have the crystals that he clearly wants for some reason, he keeps coming after us, making our lives a living hell. So, there's clearly only one solution to our issues!" Vincent paused, looking earnestly at Mark.  
Mark, playing along, responded "And dear Vincent what might that solution be?"  
"We gotta kill him!"  
If Mark had a drink, he would've spit it out everywhere. "What the hell man what happened to being tired of killing people?" Mark paused for a second, thinking back to the night before. "Although, now that you say that, I do remember something from last night. His heartbeat. It wasn't there."  
Vincent clapped his hands together, pointing his fingers at Mark. "I knew something freaky was going on with that guy! If he doesn't have a heartbeat, that means he's not alive right? Meaning, it would be a public service and morally correct to 'kill' him, because he's some kind of freako zombie that keeps turning other people into freakos."  
Mark couldn't believe it, but Vincent was somehow convincing. "Ok ok, so what's the actual plan."  
"I'm so glad you asked my naive student! So far, I've been attacked four different times, in four different spots. It's been a while, but I feel like it wouldn't hurt to go back and look at those four spots, see if there's any unique reason why he showed up there, or even just to see if there's any sort of evidence laying around."  
"Ok, now you lost me."  
"Look, it's nothing crazy, but it's a start. We gotta find out more about this guy so we can get the jump on him before he gets to one of us again."  
Mark sighed. "I guess you're right. We gotta start somewhere. By the way, how good are you at actually making things with your powers?"  
Vincent perked up, a sparkle in his eyes "What kind of things?"

---

Vincent sat on a bench, enjoying the yellowing leaves, the sounds of people's feet stomping on the gravel path. He looked at the plaque on the bench, a sadness flowing through him. "Elizabeth Winsted. 1962-2017. A daughter, a mother, wife, and most importantly, a friend to all." This was the first time Vincent had visited the park since he killed her. He remembered going to her funeral, watching them lower her coffin from afar, a piece of him buried with her. Vincent stood up, sighing, ready to give up for the day. He had spent the past four hours searching for anything, even a speck of dust that looked like it could be connected to the man in the suit, but he had come up with nothing. It was a long shot anyways. He went to call Mark to ask him how his search was going when he hurt a gut wrenching scream coming from behind him. Without hesitation, he flipped around and started charging towards the source. He rounded a corner to see a most horrifying sight. There he was, the man in the suit, holding a kid no older than 13 by the head. The boy's body was convulsing, his arms and legs limp, flopping around like a fish out of water. The man's fingers seemed to stretch impossibly long, almost covering the boy's entire head, as he stared at Vincent with that unnerving smile. The man dropped the boy, before calmly pivoting around and walking away. Vincent slammed his hands into the ground, hoping to block the man's path, but it was too late. He had already vanished into the shadows. Vincent ran over to the boy, who was unmoving on the rough ground. Vincent tried shaking him awake, yelling for help, but it was no use. He noticed something, glittering on the boy's forehead, and moved the boy's hair to examine it closer. Sitting directly in his temple laid a small, black crystal, with what appeared to be a shadow moving beneath its surface. The boy's eyes snapped open, and that familiar grin seeped across his face.

Good job at the meet yesterday, you looked great!"」Mark smiled at the text from Molly, shoving his phone back in his pocket. "Maybe she decided to stop being such a prick." He wondered to himself. To be honest, he did miss her. He missed their late night calls and spontaneous bike rides. He just missed being around her. Her beautiful sea blue eyes, her long, brown hair-  
"Do you wanna see it or not?" the girl yelled down the alleyway.  
"Sorry, just got lost in thought there," Mark replied, walking into the dark alley, out of the sun's protective light.
