The next 24 hours were a blur. When Mark arrived to the start of the trail, the police and fire department were everywhere. He saw Andrew, covered in blood, looking like he was in a trance, being escorted to an officers car. Andrew's cronies were also there, being interrogated, looking like they were about to shit their pants. Mark then noticed Tim, who was pointing directly at him, eyes wide like he had seen a ghost. Paramedics rushed over to Mark to examine him, but found no signs of injury, despite the huge splotches of blood that stained his shirt. He had to spend the next 30 minutes trying to convince the poor paramedics that he did not need to go to the hospital, and that he just wanted to get home. To be truthful, Mark wanted nothing more then to go get this stupid gem that was imbedded into him checked out and removed, but he had a feeling that he should keep it hidden until he himself figured out what it was. It didn't seem to be injuring him in anyway, and it emitted a nice warmth that he found comforting. Mark did take up the paramedics offer of fresh clothes however, as he knew if he came home with even a drop of blood on him his mother would flip. 

When he arrived at home, Mark was immediately greeted with a tackle from his dog, a small boxer mix named Nara, who was greeting him like he had just come back from war. 

"I missed you too, girl." he said as he patted the dogs head.  

He glanced over at the picture of his dad, hanging by the entrance, the man's radiant smile beaming down on his son. Mark stood up and examined the picture closely. He looked a lot like his father, same messy brown hair and deep emerald eyes. He had this look of confidence that Mark would never be able to replicate, the look of a man who was loved by everyone. Mark gently placed his hand on the portrait, thanking his father for being his guardian angel. Mark was then tackled once again, this time by his mother, who was a total mess. 

"I thought it happened again," she sobbed into his shoulder, "when I heard about the fire it all hit me like a truck, I thought-" 

"It's okay mom, " Mark interrupted, "I made it out fine, I wasn't even near the fire."

The floor boards creaked, and Mark snapped his head towards the source of the sound. Standing there was Molly, tears streaming down her face. Before Mark could say anything, she rushed past him and went right out the door. 

"That poor girl," his mom started "she was the one that told me what had happened. She could barely get the words out when she showed up at our door." 

Mark said nothing, and just hugged his mom as tight as he could. She pushed back, a look of concern on her face, "Are you getting a fever? You feel very warm." 

Despite his mothers protest, Mark insisted that he would return to school the next day, as he did not want to make a big deal of the whole situation. With his door closed, alone in his room, Mark took off his shirt and began examining the crystal. Looking at it in the mirror just freaked him out even more. The gem appeared as though it was welded to his flesh, with severe scarring and burns surrounding its perimeter. The crystal itself was relatively smooth besides some jagged edges, and was warm to the touch. It's glow had weakened compared to when he first noticed it, now emitting a light that would've only been visible in total darkness. Looking at it more closely, he noticed that under the gems surface, a large crack was split directly down the middle. Weird. He spent the rest of the night performing a series of experiments on the new addition to his body, poking and prodding away with a variety of tools. He was tempted to try and jab it with a knife, but after today's incident he thought it was better of he stayed away from sharp objects. When he took a shower, he took notice that any water that directly hit the gem was immediately turned to steam, which was strange because there was no way it was hot enough to do that, but he just accepted it, as he had the gem being attached to him in the first place. 

Somehow, Marks second day of school started off worse then his first. As he was eating breakfast, his mom casually told him that she wouldn't have to drive Molly anymore, as one of her new friends was going to take her to school now instead. *Great*. The drive to school filled him with anxiety, and he tried to blare the radio to drown out the noise, but that didn't help. The moment he stepped onto the schools property, it felt like all eyes were on him. Mark learned that rumors spread quickly in high school, with this particular rumor being that he was brutally stabbed to death by one of his fellow students, and then somehow came back from the dead without a scratch on him. I mean, it wasn't a false rumor, but everyone else didn't know that. Everywhere he went, whispers followed. His delusions from the day before had now become reality, and he had hit a new peak for his anxiety. He saw Molly in the hallway, surrounded by her new posse. They were all looking at him, except Molly of course, who kept talking like he wasn't there. It was like she was a completely different person then the girl that was in tears in his house just the day before. At lunch, he was thankfully able to find a table alone, but no one dared to sit near him. As he ate, he noticed something out of the corner of his eye. Another student, who looked like a grown man, sat alone as well, looking directly at Mark. Mark pretended not to notice, but every time he glanced back, the man was still staring, his brown eyes piercing Mark's soul. 

"I want to apologize." 

Mark snapped out of his trance to see Tim standing over him, lunch tray in hand. Mark, who was caught off guard, stammered "I should be the one apologizing to you, I should've helped you back there but panicked." 

"No trust me, it's okay, it's my fault that they were able to attack you. I do have to ask though," Tim sat down in front of Mark, shifting uncomfortably. "I don't want to sound rude, but how are you here right now? I watched him stab you before I ran off, yet you don't have so much as a bruise on your body." 

Mark thought about telling Tim the truth for a moment, but he ultimately decided against it. Instead he told him "I'm pretty sure I have some kind of guardian angel. My dad died when I was pretty young, so I liked to think that he watches over me." 

Tim didn't really seem satisfied with that answered, but he accepted it. 

Maybe it's due to how fast rumors move around high school, but by the third day, everyone pretty much stopped caring. They were still talking about Andrew, who was currently under police custody, but Mark went back to being unnoticed. He did gain a friend in Tim, who was now permanently attached to his hip. It turns out they actually had a lot in common, they liked the same kinds of video games and comics, so their friendship came naturally. As the week went on, Mark began falling into a routine. Go to school, get ignored by Molly, hang out with Tim, get watched by that creepy senior at lunch, go home, do homework, go to sleep. Eventually the police started showing up at his house to ask Mark to walk them through the incident, which Mark gladly did, leaving out the part where he came back from the dead with a stone lodged in his chest of course. By the time Friday rolled around, he was fully in the swing of things, now accepting that this random stone was just going to be part of his life now, as it literally did nothing. He thought to himself on the drive to school, "*I'll show it to my mom tonight, then we'll figure out where to go from there.*" 

The radio blared, "THIS JUST IN: WE ARE NOW RECEIVING REPORTS THAT-" 

Marks mom shut off the radio and looked at her son. "I'm very proud of you Mark, you know that right? With everything thats happened this week, you've been so strong, and you're starting to remind me of your father more and more every day."  Tears began welling up in her eyes.

"There's no need to get all sappy so early in the morning." Mark sighed back, his mom leaning over the passenger seat to give him a hug. "Hey mom, when I get home tonight, I want to talk to you about something." he said. His mom gave a worried look, so he quickly followed up "It's nothing serious, just something that's been on my mind." 

With that, he got out and waved bye to his mom. 

After what happened that Monday, any normal person would have never stepped foot on that trail again. After arguing with his coach that he was fine, Mark was back to running, and it was bliss. Now that he had Tim with him, the runs were the best part of his day. Halfway through their run, Tim stopped to use a porta-potty that they had found on the trail, and Mark took the time to catch his breath. While standing on the side of the trail, Mark heard a rustling of the leaves. He turned around, only to see a figure hobbling through the forrest. Now, at this point any normal person would have brushed it off. I think you know the drill at this point though. Mark's chest began to burn as he looked at the figure, the crystals glow increasing in brightness. He started to make his way towards the figure, trudging deep into the forrest. He lost track of the mystery man, and realized he was lost. As he turned around to make his way back to the trail, he saw him. Mark almost didn't recognize the boy, whose body was bloodies and bruised, his clothes tattered. But he knew from that smile who the figure was. Andrew held out his hand, and a dagger began hurling towards Mark.
